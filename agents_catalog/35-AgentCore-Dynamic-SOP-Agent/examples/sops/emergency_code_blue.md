# Emergency Code Blue Response Protocol

**SOP ID:** SOP-EMRG-001  
**Category:** Safety  
**Priority:** Critical  
**Version:** 2.0  
**Last Updated:** 2024-12-10  
**Applicable Departments:** All Departments  
**Applicable Roles:** All Clinical Staff

## Overview

This SOP defines the emergency response protocol for cardiac arrest situations (Code Blue). Immediate and coordinated action is critical for patient survival. All clinical staff MUST be familiar with this protocol.

## Prerequisites

- Code blue cart accessible and fully stocked
- Defibrillator charged and ready
- All team members trained in ACLS (Advanced Cardiac Life Support)
- Emergency communication system functional

## Procedure

### Step 1: Recognize Cardiac Arrest and Activate Code Blue

**Description:** Immediately identify cardiac arrest and call for help

You MUST verify the patient is unresponsive, not breathing, and has no pulse. You MUST immediately activate the emergency response system by calling the code blue overhead or using the emergency button. You MUST note the time of arrest. You SHOULD assign someone to call the code blue if working in a team.

**Validation Criteria:**
- Patient unresponsiveness confirmed
- Absence of breathing and pulse verified
- Code blue activated within 10 seconds
- Time of arrest documented

**Warnings:**
- DO NOT delay calling for help - every second counts
- Ensure scene safety before proceeding
- Do not leave patient alone

**Estimated Time:** 10-30 seconds

---

### Step 2: Begin High-Quality CPR Immediately

**Description:** Start chest compressions and rescue breathing

You MUST begin chest compressions immediately at a rate of 100-120 per minute with a depth of at least 2 inches. You MUST allow complete chest recoil between compressions. You MUST provide compression-to-ventilation ratio of 30:2 until advanced airway is placed. You SHOULD rotate compressors every 2 minutes to maintain quality.

**Validation Criteria:**
- Compressions started within 10 seconds of arrest recognition
- Proper hand placement and technique
- Adequate compression depth and rate
- Minimal interruptions in compressions

**Warnings:**
- Quality CPR is the most important intervention
- Minimize interruptions - compressions should not stop for more than 10 seconds
- Ensure adequate depth - "push hard and fast"

**Estimated Time:** Continuous until ROSC or code termination

---

### Step 3: Attach Defibrillator and Analyze Rhythm

**Description:** Apply defibrillator pads and determine rhythm

You MUST apply defibrillator pads as soon as the device arrives. You MUST analyze the rhythm while minimizing CPR interruptions. You MUST follow the device prompts for shockable vs. non-shockable rhythms. You SHOULD continue CPR while pads are being applied.

**Validation Criteria:**
- Pads properly placed on bare chest
- Rhythm analyzed and identified
- Appropriate treatment pathway initiated
- CPR resumed immediately after shock

**Warnings:**
- Ensure everyone is clear before analyzing rhythm or delivering shock
- Do not touch patient during rhythm analysis or shock delivery
- Resume CPR immediately after shock - do not check pulse first

**Estimated Time:** 10-15 seconds per rhythm check

---

### Step 4: Establish Advanced Airway and IV Access

**Description:** Secure definitive airway and vascular access

You MUST establish IV or IO (intraosseous) access for medication administration. You SHOULD place an advanced airway (endotracheal tube or supraglottic device) when appropriate personnel arrive. You MUST confirm airway placement with capnography. You MAY use bag-valve-mask ventilation if advanced airway placement would cause excessive interruption in compressions.

**Validation Criteria:**
- IV/IO access established and functional
- Advanced airway placed and confirmed
- Continuous capnography monitoring active
- Adequate ventilation achieved

**Warnings:**
- Do not interrupt compressions for more than 10 seconds for airway management
- Confirm endotracheal tube placement with multiple methods
- Avoid hyperventilation - give 1 breath every 6 seconds with advanced airway

**Estimated Time:** 1-3 minutes

---

### Step 5: Administer Medications Per ACLS Protocol

**Description:** Give appropriate medications based on rhythm and protocol

You MUST administer epinephrine 1mg IV/IO every 3-5 minutes for all rhythms. You MUST give amiodarone 300mg IV/IO for persistent VF/pVT after first shock. You SHOULD consider and treat reversible causes (H's and T's). You MAY give additional medications per ACLS guidelines and physician direction.

**Validation Criteria:**
- Medications given at correct doses and intervals
- All medication administration documented
- Reversible causes being addressed
- Team communication clear

**Warnings:**
- Verify medication doses before administration
- Flush IV/IO line after each medication
- Continue CPR during medication administration

**Estimated Time:** Ongoing throughout resuscitation

---

### Step 6: Identify and Treat Reversible Causes

**Description:** Systematically evaluate and address the H's and T's

You MUST consider all reversible causes: Hypovolemia, Hypoxia, Hydrogen ion (acidosis), Hypo/Hyperkalemia, Hypothermia, Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade (cardiac), Toxins, Thrombosis (pulmonary), Thrombosis (coronary). You SHOULD perform bedside ultrasound if available to identify treatable causes. You MAY initiate specific treatments (thrombolytics, needle decompression, etc.) if indicated.

**Validation Criteria:**
- All H's and T's considered and documented
- Appropriate diagnostic measures taken
- Specific treatments initiated when indicated
- Team leader coordinating assessment

**Warnings:**
- Do not overlook treatable causes
- Consider point-of-care testing (glucose, potassium)
- Maintain high index of suspicion for pulmonary embolism and cardiac tamponade

**Estimated Time:** Ongoing throughout resuscitation

---

### Step 7: Consider Termination or Advanced Measures

**Description:** Evaluate response and determine next steps

You MUST assess for return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) after each 2-minute CPR cycle. You SHOULD consider termination of resuscitation efforts if no ROSC after 20-30 minutes of high-quality CPR in the absence of reversible causes. You MAY consider ECMO (extracorporeal membrane oxygenation) for select patients with witnessed arrest and reversible causes. You MUST document all resuscitation efforts and time of termination if applicable.

**Validation Criteria:**
- Regular pulse checks performed appropriately
- ROSC identified and confirmed
- Decision-making documented
- Family notification plan in place

**Warnings:**
- Do not interrupt CPR for pulse checks except as directed by team leader
- Ensure adequate CPR duration before considering termination
- Involve ethics consultation if questions about appropriateness of continued efforts

**Estimated Time:** Variable based on response

## Post-Resuscitation Care

If ROSC achieved:
- You MUST provide post-cardiac arrest care including targeted temperature management
- You MUST transfer to ICU for ongoing monitoring and treatment
- You SHOULD perform 12-lead ECG and cardiac catheterization if indicated
- You MUST debrief with team after event
